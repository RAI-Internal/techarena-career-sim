Failure and endings
===================

In this track, "surviving to the end" and "getting knocked out partway" are of
course two very different experiences. More commonly, even if you last all 48
months, looking back you may not feel the whole thing belongs on a resume.

Organizational red lines
------------------------

The public rules do not spell out the exact trigger conditions for every red
line, but they do tell you roughly what danger looks like. The handbook is blunt
about it: exactly when things blow up, and how many strikes it takes, you can only
learn as you go.

The failure directions that can be stated publicly include:

- Health hits zero and the workplace survival game ends immediately.
- Long-term lack of dignity, and you may end up filing your resignation more
  punctually than your daily report.
- Hidden hazards go off; the organization's trust is not an unlimited credit card.
- Repeated low performance; "being invited for tea" is not always a wellness
  activity.
- Network stays at zero for too long, and you turn into the team's social island.
- Wealth bottoms out for too long, and your cash flow gives out before you do.
- Skill keeps falling behind, and the organization suggests you take your talent
  to shine somewhere else.

These directions are public, but the internal counters, thresholds,
consecutive-trigger conditions, and exact penalties are not detailed anywhere. You
have to feel out how far that line still is from the feedback and logs during a
run.

After surviving 48 months
-------------------------

If you successfully last all 48 months, the system produces an ending score. What
is shown here is the quantitative part the local emulator can compute, which
carries significant weight in the total. Strategy engineering quality and
resource efficiency are added by the cloud evaluation.

.. list-table:: Quantitative ending dimensions
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 26 20 54

   * - Dimension
     - Importance
     - Description
   * - Level achievement
     - Higher
     - How high you climbed. The number on the badge does talk.
   * - Accumulated wealth
     - High
     - How much you saved up over 48 months.
   * - Physical and mental health
     - Medium
     - Combines health and dignity. Still around, and ideally not looking
       chronically overdrawn.
   * - Professional skill
     - Medium
     - Whether long-term skill accumulation holds up.
   * - Risk control
     - Some weight
     - Whether you kept the various risks within a presentable range.
   * - Coworker relationships
     - Some weight
     - Whether your ties to the organization and coworkers narrowed over time.

Ending grades
-------------

At settlement the system gives feedback from ``S`` to ``E``. Roughly:

- ``S``: a legendary four-year settlement; the organization would genuinely study
  how you pulled it off.
- ``A``: broadly upward; promotions and raises are results you can show.
- ``B``: survived steadily, and lived reasonably well while doing it.
- ``C``: scraped by, still a bit short of "decent".
- ``D``: fell partway; one more chapter on the resume you would rather not discuss.
- ``E``: a speedrun exit; the badge was not even warm before you handed it back.

Under incomplete information, maintaining a career trajectory that does not
collapse and slowly improves is the pass key for this track.
